Managing Corrosion Under Insulation in Industrial Facilities

Corrosion under insulation is difficult to manage because the damage is hidden, moisture entry is uneven and broad insulation removal is disruptive. An effective programme prioritises susceptible systems and uses screening, direct examination and prevention as complementary controls.

Why CUI is unpredictable

Water can enter through damaged weather barriers, penetrations, terminations and poorly sealed fittings. Wet insulation may retain contaminants against the metal surface. Temperature cycling, marine atmosphere and coating breakdown further influence whether local attack develops. The technical basis should identify the affected equipment, assumptions, uncertainties, responsible authority and evidence required to close the resulting action.

In practical application, why cui is unpredictable should be translated into documented requirements for the relevant assets and responsible teams. For this subject, the priority is to ensure that corrosion under insulation management leads to an auditable decision, appropriate field or engineering activity, and a defined response when results fall outside the approved basis. Records should remain linked to the asset so future reviews can compare assumptions with actual condition and operating experience.

Build a susceptibility model

Review material, operating temperature, cycling, insulation type, age, coating condition, exposure, geometry and leak history. Prioritise supports, low points, nozzles, penetrations, steam tracing and areas where water can collect. Field walkdowns are important because drawings seldom show current weatherproofing condition. This keeps the work focused on a decision and prevents a completed inspection or calculation from being mistaken for a fully controlled integrity outcome.

Screening and confirmation

Pulsed eddy current, profile radiography, thermography and other techniques may help identify areas for follow-up, subject to geometry and method limitations. Direct visual examination and ultrasonic measurement after local removal remain important for confirming condition and sizing loss. Where information is incomplete, the uncertainty should be recorded and addressed through verification, conservative assumptions, additional monitoring or appropriately timed examination.

Inspection windows and coverage

Choose locations based on susceptibility rather than convenience. Record what was actually exposed and examined. Sampling plans need expansion rules so unexpected damage leads to wider investigation instead of an isolated repair. Applicable codes and standards provide important structure, but their relevance, edition and acceptance requirements must be confirmed for the asset, jurisdiction and client-approved scope.

Prevention and repair quality

Coating systems, sealed weather barriers, drainage and insulation detailing reduce recurrence. Repairs should address the moisture path as well as the corroded metal. Reinsulation before coating cure or without closure inspection can recreate the original threat. The strongest result is a traceable connection between the observed condition, its engineering significance and the action that protects continued operation.

A sustainable CUI programme

Maintain a location-based register of susceptibility, inspection results, repairs and future due dates. Integrate insulation condition into routine field surveillance and use findings to refine the model across similar systems. For UAE and GCC facilities, the final approach should also reflect local regulatory obligations, the owner’s engineering practices, environmental exposure and the practical access available during operation or shutdown.
