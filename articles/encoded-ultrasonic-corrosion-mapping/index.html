Encoded Ultrasonic Corrosion Mapping for Pressure Equipment

Technical principle and inspection purpose

Encoded corrosion mapping collects position-linked thickness data to visualise general and local metal loss across a defined surface. Data quality depends on controlled calibration, repeatable location referencing, suitable surface or access conditions and preservation of the original inspection evidence.

For ultrasonic corrosion mapping, the technical principle and inspection purpose stage should document the inspection objective, target flaw, material and component geometry. This stage must remain linked to the asset and original data so later reviewers can distinguish verified condition, interpreted indications, assumptions and areas where further evidence is required.

Where the technique creates value

It supports assessment of vessels, tanks, piping, elbows and other accessible components affected by corrosion, erosion or local thinning. Where confidence is limited, the report should recommend verification, complementary NDT, expanded coverage or engineering assessment rather than presenting an unsupported conclusion.

For ultrasonic corrosion mapping, the where the technique creates value stage should document the asset locations, access method, expected coverage and operating constraints. Planning and procedure requirements

Grid resolution, scanner stability, surface preparation, coating condition, couplant, temperature and expected pit size influence the examination design. Applicable codes and standards provide a framework, but the governing document and edition must be established for the specific asset and contract.

For ultrasonic corrosion mapping, the planning and procedure requirements stage should document the approved procedure, calibration basis, essential variables and personnel responsibilities. Important limitations and complementary methods

The displayed minimum can be affected by loss of coupling, surface roughness, laminations or data-processing settings; maps require validation and review of raw signals. For industrial facilities in the UAE and GCC, selection must also reflect operating temperature, environmental exposure, access restrictions, shutdown windows and the owner’s technical requirements.

From inspection evidence to an engineering decision

A useful deliverable provides traceable coordinates, coverage, minimum values, profiles and exportable data for remaining-strength or FFS assessment. A competent team should define the intended result before mobilisation so equipment capability, scanning coverage and reporting detail are aligned with the client’s decision.
