Why Damage Mechanism Review Must Come Before Inspection Planning

Inspection cannot be effectively planned until the team understands what damage may occur, where it is likely to appear and which technique can find it. A Damage Mechanism Review converts process and materials knowledge into an equipment-specific examination basis.

Inspection cannot be effectively planned until the team understands what damage may occur, where it is likely to appear and which technique can find it. A Damage Mechanism Review converts process and materials knowledge into an equipment-specific examination basis.

Why generic checklists fail

The same equipment type can experience different degradation in different services. A carbon-steel line carrying dry product does not have the same threat profile as a wet sour line, even if their size and age match. Technique selection based only on equipment class can miss the governing mechanism. Applicable codes and standards provide important structure, but their relevance, edition and acceptance requirements must be confirmed for the asset, jurisdiction and client-approved scope.

In practical application, why generic checklists fail should be translated into documented requirements for the relevant assets and responsible teams. For this subject, the priority is to ensure that damage mechanism review leads to an auditable decision, appropriate field or engineering activity, and a defined response when results fall outside the approved basis. Records should remain linked to the asset so future reviews can compare assumptions with actual condition and operating experience.

Inputs to the review

The team examines metallurgy, fabrication, temperature, pressure, composition, contaminants, flow, injection points, insulation, cyclic operation and excursions. Inspection and failure history test whether theoretical susceptibility is appearing in the field. The strongest result is a traceable connection between the observed condition, its engineering significance and the action that protects continued operation.

Describe mechanism and location

A useful record states the mechanism, affected material, required conditions, expected morphology, susceptible zones and likely rate or activation. It distinguishes general thinning from local corrosion and surface cracking from embedded or volumetric flaws. For UAE and GCC facilities, the final approach should also reflect local regulatory obligations, the owner’s engineering practices, environmental exposure and the practical access available during operation or shutdown.

Connect to inspection capability

Once flaw type and location are understood, the team can select surface, ultrasonic, radiographic, electromagnetic or visual methods with suitable coverage. Where no practical inspection gives adequate confidence, process control, monitoring or design change becomes more important. The technical basis should identify the affected equipment, assumptions, uncertainties, responsible authority and evidence required to close the resulting action.

Use recognised references carefully

API RP 571 is a widely used reference for damage mechanisms in refining and related process industries. It does not replace facility-specific review. Other sectors and materials may require different codes, standards, technical literature and operating experience. This keeps the work focused on a decision and prevents a completed inspection or calculation from being mistaken for a fully controlled integrity outcome.

Keep the review current

Feed changes, debottlenecking, new chemicals, altered temperatures, repairs and unexpected findings can change susceptibility. Management of change and RBI reassessment should therefore reopen the damage review when the service basis changes. Where information is incomplete, the uncertainty should be recorded and addressed through verification, conservative assumptions, additional monitoring or appropriately timed examination.
