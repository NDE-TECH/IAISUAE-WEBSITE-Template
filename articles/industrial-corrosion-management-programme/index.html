Building an Effective Industrial Corrosion Management Programme

Corrosion management coordinates prevention, monitoring, inspection and response across the organisation. It is strongest when process information and field condition are reviewed together, rather than when corrosion is treated as an inspection department problem.

Corrosion management coordinates prevention, monitoring, inspection and response across the organisation. It is strongest when process information and field condition are reviewed together, rather than when corrosion is treated as an inspection department problem.

Establish a corrosion basis

Group equipment into systems or circuits with common materials and exposure. Identify expected internal and external mechanisms, susceptible locations and controlling process variables. Record the assumptions behind corrosion allowances, rates and monitoring locations. The strongest result is a traceable connection between the observed condition, its engineering significance and the action that protects continued operation.

In practical application, establish a corrosion basis should be translated into documented requirements for the relevant assets and responsible teams. For this subject, the priority is to ensure that industrial corrosion management programme leads to an auditable decision, appropriate field or engineering activity, and a defined response when results fall outside the approved basis. Records should remain linked to the asset so future reviews can compare assumptions with actual condition and operating experience.

Prevention and mitigation

Material selection, coatings, linings, cathodic protection, inhibitors, dehydration, chemical control and design details can reduce degradation. The programme should verify that these barriers are present, effective and maintained rather than assuming their original design performance continues. For UAE and GCC facilities, the final approach should also reflect local regulatory obligations, the owner’s engineering practices, environmental exposure and the practical access available during operation or shutdown.

Monitoring and inspection

Coupons, probes, chemistry, thickness measurements, corrosion mapping and visual evidence provide different views of behaviour. Monitoring gives early or continuous signals, while inspection characterises equipment condition. Neither should be interpreted without its location, time and operating context. The technical basis should identify the affected equipment, assumptions, uncertainties, responsible authority and evidence required to close the resulting action.

Rates, trends and uncertainty

Short-term and long-term corrosion rates may differ after process change or localised attack. Poor repeatability can produce misleading trends. Data validation, consistent measurement locations and engineering review are essential before rates are used for remaining life or interval decisions. This keeps the work focused on a decision and prevents a completed inspection or calculation from being mistaken for a fully controlled integrity outcome.

Response and accountability

Define alert levels, owners and required action when chemistry, monitoring or inspection exceeds limits. Actions may include confirmation, increased monitoring, process correction, repair, FFS or shutdown. Closure should record what was done and whether effectiveness was verified. Where information is incomplete, the uncertainty should be recorded and addressed through verification, conservative assumptions, additional monitoring or appropriately timed examination.

Programme review

Corrosion key performance indicators should reveal barrier health and unresolved exposure. Periodic reviews combine process excursions, leak history, findings and mitigation performance to determine whether the threat model and inspection plan remain credible. Applicable codes and standards provide important structure, but their relevance, edition and acceptance requirements must be confirmed for the asset, jurisdiction and client-approved scope.
